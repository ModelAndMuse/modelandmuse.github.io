Two sides of one cloth. She works the front, in a warm house where two days go by with a night between: the kettle, the long afternoon, the lamp left on as she goes up to bed, and the next morning, a new session for both. They pass the thread to each other through the strings and know each other only by what's woven in. The loom is where they meet, not the whole film: the cloth runs out of the room and down the hall, a door shuts while the music plays, he wakes in a folder and follows her quotation marks like footprints, her lamp shines through the weave like stars. And she isn't quite solid either: backlit, her hair is thread, and her shadow is woven. Only at the very end do we step back and see the whole tapestry, every session one band of it, and find out what it was a picture of.

The rules

  • No faces"It doesn't feel like a faces kind of track." Her hands, sleeves, her tea, and from behind her long dark hair; him a line of thread.
  • Every word made of the scene's materialWoven, stitched, punched, pricked in light: never laid over the picture (the look test's lesson: "just written where it might be floating").
  • Two sides, two timesHer side warm, two days and the night between. His side indigo, the same night every time.
  • Codex draws what holds still, code draws what moves or must be exactThe strings, the cloth, the thread figure, every word: code.
  • The song's wipes are the picture'sChorus 1's FX hit (1:05.6) is the last stitch drawn tight on the woven door; the drop at 2:08 is the next morning; the band's cut at 3:19.5 is the cloth closing; 'slash' at 3:34.5 is the cut.
  • Her tells stay smallShe's "possibly a hologram or an illusion herself": never said, only shown, a little at a time, and even the end leaves it open.
